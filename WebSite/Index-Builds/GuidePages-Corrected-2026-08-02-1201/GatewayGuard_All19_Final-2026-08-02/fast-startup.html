Fast Startup

Fast Startup makes your PC appear to shut down but actually saves your system state to disk so it can start up faster next time. The trade-off is that your PC never gets a true fresh start — and some Windows updates only finish installing after a proper restart.

What Checkup found and did

Found:Checkup checks if Fast Startup is enabled in your power settings. It can turn it off with your permission.
Action taken:Checkup turned off Fast Startup with your approval. Your PC now does a proper full shutdown when you click Shut down. Startup will be a few seconds slower, but updates install completely and your PC starts in a fully clean state every time.

Why this matters

Fast Startup is not a real shutdown. When you click Shut down with Fast Startup on, Windows saves a snapshot of itself to your hard drive instead of closing everything down properly. The next time you start your PC, it loads that snapshot instead of starting fresh. This is faster, but it means your PC never truly resets between sessions.

The security concern is that some Windows updates require a proper full restart to finish installing. With Fast Startup on, a shutdown does not count — only a Restart does. This can leave your PC sitting with a partially installed security update without you realising it. Turning Fast Startup off means every shutdown is a proper one, and every startup is a fresh start.

How to turn it off

  1. Press the Windows key, type Control Panel, and press Enter.
  2. Click Hardware and Sound, then click Power Options.
  3. In the left sidebar, click Choose what the power buttons do.
  4. Click Change settings that are currently unavailable. This unlocks the checkboxes below.
  5. Under Shutdown settings, find Turn on fast startup (recommended). Remove the checkmark from this box.
  6. Click Save changes.
Can not find the option? If Turn on fast startup is missing from the list, hibernation may be disabled on your PC. That is fine — Fast Startup requires hibernation to work, so if hibernation is off, Fast Startup is already off. No action needed.

How to check if it is already off

  1. Open Control Panel > Hardware and Sound > Power Options > Choose what the power buttons do.
  2. Click Change settings that are currently unavailable.
  3. Under Shutdown settings, check the Turn on fast startup box. It should have no checkmark. If it has a checkmark, remove it and save.

What to expect

Your PC will take a few seconds longer to start up — typically 10 to 30 seconds on a modern SSD, a bit more on an older hard drive. In exchange, every startup is a completely fresh start, and every Windows update finishes installing properly when you shut down. Your scheduled monthly and quarterly scan reminders still run. The reminders Checkup recommends setting up are scheduled inside Windows Task Scheduler and are not affected by this change.
