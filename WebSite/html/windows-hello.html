Windows Hello

Windows Hello lets you sign in with a PIN, fingerprint, or face instead of a password. It is faster, more convenient, and harder for someone else to use — a PIN only works on your specific PC, unlike a password that works anywhere.

Why this matters

A PIN is safer than a password for signing into Windows. That sounds backwards, but here is why it is true: your password works on any device, anywhere — if someone learns it, they can use it from the other side of the world. A Windows Hello PIN only works on your specific PC. It never leaves your device and is never sent over the internet. If someone learns your PIN, they still cannot use it without physical access to your computer.

Fingerprint and face sign-in go one step further — only you can use them. They are also faster than typing a password every time you sit down.

How to set it up

  1. Press the Windows key, type Sign-in options, and press Enter.
  2. You will see options for Windows Hello PIN, Fingerprint recognition, and Facial recognition. At minimum, set up a PIN — it is available on every PC.
  3. Click Windows Hello PIN, then click Add.
  4. Windows will ask you to verify your Microsoft account password first. Enter it and click OK.
  5. Choose a PIN of at least 6 digits. Avoid obvious patterns like 123456 or your birth year. Click OK.
  6. Done. Next time you sign in, Windows will show a PIN prompt instead of a password prompt.
Also set up fingerprint or face if your PC supports it. Check under Fingerprint recognition or Facial recognition in the same Sign-in options screen. If your PC has a fingerprint reader or front camera with infrared support, these options will be available. If they are grayed out, your PC hardware does not support them — the PIN is sufficient.

How to check if it is already set up

  1. Press Windows key, type Sign-in options, press Enter.
  2. If Windows Hello PIN shows a Change button, a PIN is already set up. If it shows Add, it is not set up yet.

What to expect

Once set up, Windows will use your PIN or biometric by default at the sign-in screen. You can still sign in with your password if needed by clicking Sign-in options on the lock screen. Your PIN and fingerprint data never leave your PC — they are stored in a secure chip on your device, not in Microsoft’s servers.

Forgot your PIN? On the sign-in screen, click I forgot my PIN. Windows will verify your Microsoft account password and let you set a new one. You will not lose any files or settings.
