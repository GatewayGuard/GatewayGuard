Remote Desktop

Remote Desktop lets someone connect to your PC over the internet and control it as if they were sitting in front of it. If you do not use this feature, leaving it on opens a door that does not need to be open.

✎ You set this up

What Checkup found and did

Found:Checkup reviews the setting that allows inbound connections from other computers. On Windows 11 Pro, it can disable Remote Desktop with your permission. Windows 11 Home does not have this setting. Outbound connections to other devices are handled separately and are not affected.
Action taken:Checkup flagged Remote Desktop if it was on and asked for your approval to turn it off. If you use Remote Desktop intentionally — for example, to connect to your home PC from work — you can leave it on. No changes are made without your approval.

Why this matters

An open remote-access path is a security hole attackers actively look for. If your PC can be reached and controlled remotely, and someone guesses or steals your password, they are in. They can see your screen, open your files, and do anything you can do on your own machine — from anywhere in the world.

If your machine can be reached remotely and also has Remote Desktop or any other remote-access method enabled, this combination is a security hole attackers specifically scan for. Turning it off prevents this entirely. If you have never used Remote Desktop, you do not need it — and if you are not sure that you use it, you almost certainly do not.

How to turn it off

  1. Press the Windows key, type Remote Desktop settings, and press Enter.
  2. Click the toggle next to Remote Desktop to turn it Off.
  3. Click Confirm when Windows asks if you are sure.
  4. No restart required. Remote Desktop is off immediately.
Windows 11 Home users: nothing can connect in to your PC. This setting does not exist on your PC, and you do not need to do anything here. Remote Desktop is the Windows feature that lets another computer connect in to yours, and Windows 11 Home does not have it. There is no setting to leave on by mistake, and nothing sitting there waiting for a connection — not from a stranger, and not from Microsoft.
The one thing that can still let someone in is you. Separate helper programs — Quick Assist, which comes with Windows, and others such as TeamViewer — let someone see or control your screen, but only if you say yes at the time. They cannot start on their own and nobody can use them without your permission. That is what the scam call is for: someone claims to be from Microsoft and needs to connect to fix a problem. If you say yes, they are in — not because your PC is unprotected, but because you let them in. Microsoft will never call you. Hang up.

How to check if it is already off

  1. Press Windows key, type Remote Desktop settings, press Enter.
  2. The toggle should show Off. If it shows On, follow the steps above to turn it off.

What if I actually use Remote Desktop?

If you use Remote Desktop to connect to your home PC from another location, you can leave it on — but take these steps to reduce the risk:

  1. Make sure your Windows account has a strong password — at least 12 characters with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols.
  2. In Remote Desktop settings, click Advanced settings and confirm Network Level Authentication is on. It should say On. If it does not, turn it on.
  3. Under Remote Desktop users, confirm only your own account is listed. If you see any account you do not recognise, remove it by clicking it and selecting Remove.
Remote support scam warning: If anyone calls you unsolicited and asks you to turn on Remote Desktop or install remote-access software, hang up. Legitimate support staff from Microsoft, your bank, or your internet provider will never call you and ask for remote access to your PC.

Can you use Remote Desktop to connect out to another PC?

Yes. Windows 11 Home includes an app called Remote Desktop Connection that connects out to another computer — a work computer, for example. You may see it on your PC and wonder if it is a problem. It is not: it connects out, and it does not let anything connect in.

Our recommendation: if you do not use it, leave it off. If it is already off, leave it alone — there is nothing to turn off. If you use it for work, carry on. Either way, nothing can connect in to this PC, unless you let them. Never let them unless you started the contact yourself.

Do not install other remote-access programs unless you specifically need one and went looking for it yourself. Never install one because someone contacted you and asked you to.
