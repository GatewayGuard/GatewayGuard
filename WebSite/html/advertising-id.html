Advertising ID

Windows assigns your PC a unique tracking number that apps use to follow your activity and show you targeted ads. Turning it off removes that number and stops apps from tracking you across different programs.

▶ Your decision

What Checkup found and did

Found:Checkup reviews your Advertising ID setting, which lets apps show you personalized ads. It can turn that off with your permission.
Action taken:Checkup turned off the Advertising ID with your approval. This is a privacy preference — it has no effect on your PC’s security or performance. You can turn it back on at any time.

Why you might want this off

The Advertising ID is a tracking number Microsoft assigns to your PC. Apps that use it can see what you do across different programs and use that information to target you with ads. Think of it like a loyalty card number — except you did not sign up for it, and the data goes to advertisers you have never heard of.

Turning it off does not stop you from seeing ads. It just means the ads you see are not based on tracking what you do on your own PC. This is a privacy preference, not a security requirement — there is no right or wrong answer here.

How to turn it off

  1. Press the Windows key, type Privacy settings, and press Enter.
  2. In the left sidebar, click General.
  3. Find Let apps use advertising ID to make ads more interesting to you based on your app activity. The toggle should be Off. If it is On, click it to turn it Off.

How to turn it back on if you want it

  1. Press the Windows key, type Privacy settings, press Enter.
  2. Click General in the left sidebar.
  3. Toggle Let apps use advertising ID to On.

What to expect

With the Advertising ID off, apps can no longer track your activity across programs to serve targeted ads. Your PC works exactly the same in every other way. No restart is needed. If you use apps from the Microsoft Store, you may notice ads become less targeted over time — that is the expected result.

This is a privacy preference, not a security requirement. Checkup recommends turning it off as a general privacy improvement, but leaving it on does not put your PC at risk. The choice is yours.
