Firewall & Network Protection

The Windows Firewall monitors traffic coming into and going out of your PC over the network. When it’s on, it blocks uninvited connections — programs trying to reach your PC that you didn’t ask for.

What Checkup found and did

Action taken:If any profile was off, Checkup flagged it and offered to turn it on. No changes are made without your approval.

Why this matters

The firewall is the gatekeeper for your network connection. Without it, other devices on your network — or programs already on your PC — can send and receive data without any filtering. On a home network that’s usually low-risk. On a public Wi-Fi network at a coffee shop or library, it matters a lot more.

Windows has three firewall profiles because your PC behaves differently depending on where it connects. All three should be on.

How to check it yourself

  1. Open Windows Security and click Firewall & network protection.
  2. You’ll see three entries: Domain network, Private network, and Public network.
  3. Click each one and confirm the firewall toggle is set to On.
  4. The active network (the one your PC is currently connected to) will be labeled (active). That is the one to check right now.

How to change it yourself

  1. Click the profile that is off (Domain, Private, or Public).
  2. Click the toggle to turn the firewall On.
  3. Repeat for any other profiles that are off.
  4. No restart required.

What to expect

No restart required. The firewall is active immediately for new connections. Programs that were already running are not affected.

The firewall is separate from your antivirus. Defender handles malware scanning; the firewall handles network traffic. Both should be on at the same time — they protect against different things.
