Wake on LAN (explained fully below)

This setting lets another computer turn your PC on from across your home network, while your PC is asleep. It was built for offices. Almost no home user needs it, and leaving it on keeps a door open that nobody is watching.

▶ Your decision — GatewayGuard asked before changing it

Why this matters

Wake on LAN and Remote Desktop are the two “let someone in from outside” features on a home PC. We strongly recommend both be off. (LAN is just a name for your home network — the wired and wireless connections between the machines in your house.)

This setting exists so an office can turn on hundreds of computers overnight to install updates. That is a real need — in an office. At home, it means your PC can be woken by something other than you, and a PC that can be woken is a PC that can be reached when you are not sitting in front of it.

On its own, this does not let anyone into your PC. It is the combination that matters: a machine that can be turned on remotely, plus any other way in, is worth far more to an attacker than either one alone. Turning it off costs you nothing if you never used it — and if you are reading this page, you almost certainly never used it.

The phone call you should expect one day

If anyone ever phones you and asks you to turn remote access on — hang up.

The same goes for anyone who asks you to install a program so they can “connect to your computer and fix it.” It does not matter if they say they are from Microsoft, from your bank, from your internet provider, or from a company whose name you recognise.

This is the single most common scam used against home computer users today. Microsoft does not phone people. Your bank does not need to control your PC. A real company will never call you out of the blue and ask for remote access.

Hang up. If you are worried it might have been genuine, look up the company’s real number yourself — on your bank card, on a statement, on their official website — and call them back on that number. Never a number the caller gave you.

How to check it, and turn it off

  1. Press the Windows key, type Device Manager, and open it.
  2. Click the arrow next to Network adapters to open the list. (A network adapter is the part of your PC that connects it to the internet. Most PCs have two — one for Wi-Fi, one for the cable socket.)
  3. Right-click the first one and choose Properties.
  4. Click the Power Management tab. If there is no tab with that name, this one cannot wake your PC and there is nothing to change — close it and go to the next one.
  5. Find Allow this device to wake the computer. The box should be empty. If there is a tick in it, click it to remove the tick, then click OK.
  6. Repeat for your other network adapter. Both need doing — turning off one and not the other leaves the door open.

No restart is needed. The change works straight away. Here is the path if you would rather paste it than type it:

Device Manager > Network adapters > Properties > Power Management

Do I need to change anything in the startup screen settings?

No. The Windows setting above is the one that matters, and it is enough.

You may read elsewhere that this feature also has a setting in the settings screen that appears before Windows starts. That is true on some PCs, but it does not override what you just did — with the Windows box unticked, your PC will not be woken from the network. You do not need to go near those settings, and we do not recommend it. They differ on every make of PC and a wrong change there is much harder to undo.

If you actually use this feature

Some people do use it on purpose — for example, turning on a PC in another room from a phone app so it is ready when they get there. If that is you, leave it on. This is a recommendation, not a requirement, which is why Checkup asked instead of just changing it.

If you keep it on, make it safer. In the same Power Management tab, look for Only allow a magic packet to wake the computer and put a tick in that box. A magic packet is a specific “wake up” message meant only for your PC. With that ticked, ordinary network chatter can no longer wake it — only a deliberate signal will.
A note on Fast Startup. GatewayGuard also turns off Fast Startup (Setting 18), for reasons explained on that page. The two settings interact on some PCs. If you keep Wake on LAN on and it stops working reliably, the two are worth looking at together.

How to undo it

Go back to the same place — Device Manager, Network adapters, right-click the adapter, Properties, Power Management — and put the tick back in Allow this device to wake the computer. Nothing is lost by turning it off and back on.

Device Manager > Network adapters > Properties > Power Management

What to expect after

Your PC still wakes up normally. Opening the lid, pressing the power button, moving the mouse, or pressing a key all work exactly as before. The only thing that stops is another machine waking it — which is the point.

Your scheduled reminders still run. The monthly and quarterly scan reminders Checkup set up are scheduled inside Windows and do not depend on this setting.
