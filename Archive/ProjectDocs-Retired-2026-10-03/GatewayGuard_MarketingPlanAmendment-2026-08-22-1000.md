## 2. WHAT THE PRICING LOCK DID TO SECTION 7

Section 7 lists six open decisions. **Two are no longer open, and one is
worded against a state of the world that ended on 2026-08-21.**

### 2a. Decision 4 — rewrite

Currently:

> **Annual updates — delivery mechanism.** Priced at $12.99/yr in materials, undefined in practice. `FP-21`, still open

**"Priced in materials" is no longer what is true.** $12.99 is a locked
decision with a decision record behind it, and three multi-copy prices derive
from it. What remains open is narrower and should be described as such.

**Replacement:**

| # | Decision | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Annual updates — delivery mechanism.** Price is settled: $12.99/yr for 1 PC, locked 2026-08-21, with 3, 5 and 10-PC rates derived. **How a buyer actually receives and pays for the update is not.** `FP-21`, still open | The website now sells it. A price with no delivery mechanism behind it is a promise the store cannot keep |

### 2b. Decision 6 — close

Currently:

> **Footer wording** — the two lines in section 2. Live on the site today

**Both footer lines are corrected.** `CloudRequest-PricingCopy` section 2 states
it plainly and instructs that the footer not be touched.

**Replacement — move to a closed row, or strike:**

| # | Decision | State |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | ~~Footer wording~~ | **CLOSED 2026-08-21.** Both lines corrected. Live line: *"One-time purchase, yours to keep. Annual updates are optional."* |

### 2c. Add one decision that is missing

Multi-year pre-pay is now on the critical path for the website and appears
nowhere in section 7.

**Add:**

| # | Decision | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Multi-year pre-pay — which terms exist, what each costs, and how "10% per year" applies** (flat, or deepening with the term) | The pricing copy is written and held. It cannot go live without one reading of the discount rule, and the plans block on the index is incomplete until it does |

### 2d. Decision 2 is the blocker and should read like one

Decision 2, the refund policy, sits in a flat table alongside five items of
which two can ship after launch. `ReviewOfCloudDrafts-2026-08-18` already made
this point and it did not carry.

**Add beneath the section 7 table:**

> **Decisions 1 and 2 are not peers of the rest.** Decision 1 shapes everything
> in section 4. **Decision 2 is the only one that stops a store opening** — and
> it has become more pressing, not less. The plan's promise to the skeptical
> buyer is that they can read every line after purchase and return it if they
> do not like what they find. **That promise has no policy behind it**, and the
> site now asks the same buyer for money a second time each year. Gumroad may
> override a seller's own policy within its refund window, so the policy has to
> be written knowing that.

## 3. SECTION 5, CHANNEL 4 — the press answer needs one line added

The reviewer answer on source availability is good and stays as written. **It
is now incomplete in one respect:** a reviewer who is told the code ships
readable will look at the price, see a yearly update, and ask the obvious
follow-up.

**Add beneath the existing quoted answer:**

> **And be ready for the second question, which is about the yearly update.**
> The answer is equally short: *"The tool is bought once and it never expires.
> Windows security changes most years, so we offer an updated version each
> year for those who want to stay current. Nobody has to take it, and nothing
> stops if they do not."* **Say this before being asked.** A reviewer who
> discovers a recurring charge on their own writes a different paragraph than
> one who was told about it up front.
