## THE THIRTEEN QUESTIONS

### 1. Does a no-questions policy create a warranty we did not intend?

Section 6 makes five limited promises and Section 8 disclaims the rest.
Section 9 now offers a refund if the product *"is not what you expected."*

**Does "not what you expected" import a fitness-for-purpose standard through
the back door**, and does it sit badly beside the "as is" language in Section
8? If so, the fix is probably a sentence saying the refund is offered as a
matter of policy and is not an admission or a warranty. **Ask whether that
sentence helps or whether it just draws attention to the problem.**

### 2. Chargebacks, and the risk to the only sales channel

***Sourced:*** customers can file a chargeback regardless of any stated
policy, and repeated successful disputes can put a seller account at risk of
suspension.

**GatewayGuard has one sales channel and no fallback at launch. Account
suspension is an existential event, not an inconvenience.**

**Does that argue for a policy more generous than the legal minimum — which is
what we have chosen — or is there a structural protection we are missing?**
This is the question with the largest downside in the whole set.

### 3. Gumroad as merchant of record — the root question

This has been on the written-response list since 2026-08-04 and has never
been asked. **It sits underneath questions 4 and 5 and may dissolve both.**

*Sourced:* Gumroad states it is treated as the seller for indirect tax
purposes and that it handles buyer refund requests, chargebacks and disputes.

**Three parts:**
- If Gumroad is seller of record, **whose terms bind the buyer — ours or
  Gumroad's?**
- **Does our licence attach at all to a Gumroad sale?**
- **What authority do we actually have over a refund** we did not initiate?

### 4. Gumroad can refund over our heads, for longer than we say

*Sourced:* Gumroad reserves the right to issue refunds within **90 days** at
its own discretion, to head off chargebacks. Our policy says 30.

**So the agreement describes something we do not fully control.** Section 9
now says so in a sentence. **Is that sentence sufficient, or does the
agreement need to be explicit that Gumroad's terms govern the payment and ours
govern the licence?**

### 5. What happens to the licence on a Gumroad-initiated refund?

Ours ends when *we* refund. **If Gumroad refunds on day 75 without asking us,
does the licence end then too?** As drafted it is not clear. **This is a real
gap, not a hypothetical** — Gumroad's discretion is documented.

### 6. The product is readable source code. A refund cannot un-give it.

**This is the unusual fact and he needs it stated plainly.** Checkup ships as
plaintext PowerShell, deliberately, because auditability is the selling point.
**A refunded customer keeps a working, readable copy.**

We have chosen to accept that. **The question is whether the agreement should
say anything about it at all.** Section 9 asks the buyer to delete their
copies and openly admits we cannot check. **Is that admission wise or unwise?**
It is honest and it fits the brand, but he may see it as volunteering that the
term is unenforceable.

### 7. Section 13 now contradicts Section 9

**Section 13, Ending This License:** *"Ending the license does not entitle you
to a refund except as described in Section 9."*

That wording was written when Section 9 was the narrow rule with three named
exceptions. **Section 9 no longer has exceptions to point at.** As it stands, a
customer whose licence was terminated for breach appears to be pointed at an
unconditional 30-day refund right.

**This is not a wording cleanup.** The settled decision records that *"no
questions asked earns its keep only if there are none. A single hedge undoes
the whole sentence."* **A Section 13 carve-out is that hedge.** Which loss is
worse — the contradiction, or the hedge?

### 8. Multi-PC packs — is a refund all or nothing?

The packs run to 10 PCs at $79.99. **If a buyer wants a refund on a 5-PC pack
after installing on two, is that a full refund, a partial one, or nothing?**
The agreement is silent.

**Our suggestion is full refund, all or nothing, on the same 30 days** —
partial refunds need pro-rata arithmetic, a support conversation, and a way to
revoke two licences we have no way to revoke. **But it should be a decision,
not a silence.**

### 9. Bundle refunds

**New since he last read the agreement.** The bundle is one Checkup plus one
Guide at $29.99 and **has a price but no defined terms.**

**Can a buyer refund the bundle and keep the Guide?** They keep both files
either way. Same structural problem as question 8, different product.

### 10. The annual update — does 30 days work for a renewal?

Annual updates are $12.99 a year. **A renewal is delivered immediately and is
worth less than the original purchase.**

**Does the same 30-day window apply? And can a buyer refund an update and keep
using the version they already had?** Presumably yes, since the old version
still runs. **Confirm that is intended**, because it means an update can be
tried and returned every year.

### 11. Arbitration — carried from 2026-08-04, never answered

One of six clauses raised on 2026-08-04. Only severability was answered.

**Arbitration is the dispute path when a refund is refused**, which is why it
belongs in this call rather than a later one. **For a $19.99 consumer download
sold to non-technical home users, is an arbitration clause worth having, or is
it overhead that a court would not enforce against a consumer anyway?**

### 12. A mechanism for changing the terms — carried from 2026-08-04

Also from the same list, also unanswered. **The refund policy is the term most
likely to change**, and there is currently no way to change it for people who
have already bought.

**What does that mechanism look like for a product with no account system and
no login?**

### 13. The liability cap, revisited

Answered on 2026-08-04 as negligible exposure, not strictly enforceable, but
worth keeping for the deterrent.

**Worth revisiting now because the cap and the refund have converged.** The cap
is *"the amount you actually paid."* A no-questions refund hands back exactly
that. **For an ordinary dissatisfied buyer, does the refund make the cap
redundant — and does offering the cap's full value voluntarily weaken it as a
limit in a case where real damage is claimed?**

## NO CALL TIME NEEDED — WRITTEN ANSWER IS FINE

**A. Maine law on digital refunds — the original question, still unanswered.**
The 2026-08-04 draft asked *"what Maine law requires, if anything, for a
digital software product sold to consumers."* **It has never been answered.** A
30-day no-questions policy is almost certainly more generous than anything
required, **but "almost certainly" is not an answer and this is the second
consult.**

**B. Two answers from 2026-08-04 that were never finished.** Ours to ask, and
they cost about ninety seconds:
- The answer recorded on minimums was *"no minimum, we can establish"* — **a
  minimum of what?** Purchase quantity, licence term, or price?
- One answer was referred to LegalZoom with the note **"qualify in terms of"**
  and the sentence stops there. **What was the qualification?**

**C. EU and UK buyers.** *Sourced:* buyers there often hold a 14-day
withdrawal right unless it is waived at checkout for an instant download.
**A 30-day no-questions policy is strictly more generous than a 14-day
statutory right, so it appears to satisfy it without special handling.**
Confirm that reading. **The prior question of whether Gumroad captures the
waiver may be moot** — and whether to sell to the EU and UK at all is a Gumroad
setting, not a legal question.
