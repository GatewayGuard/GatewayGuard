# PART A — GUMROAD

**Everything here turns on one thing nobody has established: what Gumroad
legally *is* in our sales.**

### A1. Is Gumroad the merchant of record, and what does that make us?

**The root question. Answer this and A2 to A4 mostly follow.**

Gumroad takes the payment, issues the receipt, and can refund at its own
discretion. **We have never established whether it sells to the customer as
principal, with us as its supplier, or acts as our agent.**

- Which is it, on Gumroad's current terms?
- **If Gumroad is the merchant of record, whose refund policy actually governs
  the buyer** — ours, theirs, or both?
- Does that change who carries the sales-tax obligation, and who the buyer's
  counterparty is if they sue?

### A2. Gumroad can refund for longer than we promise. What do we do about it?

*Sourced:* Gumroad **reserves the right to issue refunds within 90 days at its
own discretion**, to head off chargebacks. **Our policy says 30.**

Section 9 now discloses this in one sentence *(excerpt C-4)*.

- **Is disclosure enough**, or must the agreement say plainly that Gumroad's
  terms govern the payment and ours govern the licence?
- Does promising 30 days, while a third party may grant 90, create any exposure
  to a buyer who is refused at day 45 by us and then granted by Gumroad?

### A3. What happens to the licence when Gumroad refunds and we did not?

**A live gap, not hypothetical.** Section 9 says the licence ends *"when we
refund you"* *(excerpt C-4)*.

- **If Gumroad refunds on day 75 without asking us, does the licence end?**
- As drafted it does not. **Should it?** And can it, when the person keeps a
  readable copy of the product either way?

### A4. Chargebacks, and the risk to the only channel we have

**The highest-stakes question in this document, and it is commercial as much as
legal.**

*Sourced:* excessive chargebacks can put a seller's account at risk. **Gumroad
is our only sales channel.** Losing it stops the business, not just a sale.

- **Does a 30-day no-questions policy reduce chargeback risk**, as we believe,
  by giving an unhappy buyer an easy path that is not their card issuer?
- Is there anything in how we word or place the policy that would strengthen us
  in a chargeback dispute?
- **What is the practical exposure if the account is suspended?** Should there
  be a second channel before launch, or is that a post-launch problem?

# PART B — OUR REFUND POLICY

### B1. Does "not what you expected" create a warranty we did not intend?

**The expensive one.** Section 8 disclaims fitness for purpose *(excerpt C-3)*.
Section 9 refunds when the product *"is not what you expected"* *(C-4)*.

- **Do those fight each other?** Does "not what you expected" import a
  fitness-for-purpose standard through the back door?
- If so, does a sentence saying the refund is offered **as a matter of policy,
  and is not an admission or a warranty** fix it — **or does it just draw
  attention to the tension?**

### B2. Section 13 now points at a Section 9 that has no exceptions

*(excerpts C-4 and C-5.)*

Section 13: *"Ending the license does not entitle you to a refund except as
described in Section 9."* **That was written when Section 9 was a
sales-are-final rule with three named exceptions. It no longer has any.**

- **As drafted, is a customer terminated for breach on day 10 pointed at an
  unconditional 30-day refund right?**
- If a carve-out is needed, **how is it worded without wrecking "no questions
  asked"?** Our own decision record says *"a single hedge undoes the whole
  sentence."* **We would rather pay the occasional bad-faith refund than
  publish a policy with an asterisk** — confirm that is a defensible position.

### B3. A refunded buyer keeps a working, readable copy

**The unusual fact, and the attorney needs it stated plainly.** Checkup ships
as a plain-text PowerShell script — deliberately, because auditability is the
selling point. **A refund cannot un-give it.**

Section 9 asks the buyer to delete their copies and **openly admits we cannot
check** *(excerpt C-4)*.

- **Is that admission wise or unwise?** It is honest and it fits the brand. Does
  it volunteer that the term is unenforceable?
- Is there better wording that stays honest without conceding the point?

### B4. Multi-PC packs and the bundle — is a refund all or nothing?

**The agreement is silent on both.**

- **Packs run to $79.99 for 10 PCs.** If a buyer installs on two and asks for a
  refund, is that full, partial, or nothing?
- **The bundle** (Checkup + Guide, $29.99) has a price and no defined terms.
  Can a buyer refund half of it?
- **Our instinct is all-or-nothing on the same 30 days**, because a partial
  refund needs pro-rata arithmetic and a way to revoke licences we have no way
  to revoke. **Is all-or-nothing defensible, and does it need to be stated?**

### B5. The annual update — does a 30-day window work for a renewal?

Annual updates are **$12.99 a year**, delivered immediately.

- Does the same 30 days apply to a renewal?
- **Can a buyer refund an update and keep using the version they already had?**
  Presumably yes, since the old version still runs — **which means the update
  can be tried and returned every year.** Confirm that is intended and
  acceptable.

### C-2. Section 2 — the multi-PC packs *(extract)*

> **If you bought a multi-PC pack.** A 3-PC, 5-PC, or 10-PC pack covers that
> many computers. Each one is noted separately, the same way, and the same
> no-charge move applies to each.

*Relevant to B4.*

### C-3. Section 8 — What GatewayGuard Does Not Promise *(extract)*

> Except for the warranties in Section 6, Checkup and the Guide are provided
> **"as is."** We do not promise that Checkup will make your computer immune to
> all security threats, catch all malware, or prevent all attacks. No security
> tool can make that guarantee.

*Relevant to B1 — this is what "not what you expected" may fight.*

### C-4. Section 9 — Refunds *(complete, and the heart of the consult)*

> Checkup and the Guide are downloadable files. **If either one is not what you
> expected, write to us within 30 days of buying and we will refund you in
> full. You do not have to give a reason.**
>
> You do not need to prove anything, send us a log file, or let us try to fix
> the problem first. If you would like to tell us what went wrong we are glad
> to hear it, because it is how the product improves — but it is not a
> condition of your refund.
>
> To ask for a refund, email support@gatewayguard.co from the address you
> bought with, or use the refund link in your Gumroad receipt. Give us your
> order number. We aim to answer within two business days.
>
> **When we refund you, your license ends** and you should delete the copies
> you have, including any backup. **We are aware we cannot check this, and we
> are not going to try.** We are asking you to be straight with us, in the same
> way we are being straight with you.
>
> **If you bought through Gumroad, Gumroad handles the payment and may also
> issue a refund under its own policy, which can run longer than our 30 days.**
> Nothing here takes away any right you have under the consumer law where you
> live.

### C-5. Section 13 — Ending This License *(complete)*

> If you break these terms, your license ends immediately and you must delete
> every copy of the GatewayGuard products you have. You may end this license
> yourself at any time by deleting every copy. **Ending the license does not
> entitle you to a refund except as described in Section 9.**

*Relevant to B2. **This is the only cross-reference to Section 9 in the whole
agreement.***

### C-6. Section 10 — Liability Limit *(context only)*

> Our total liability to you for any claim related to GatewayGuard products is
> limited to **the amount you actually paid** for the product involved.

*Included because a full refund equals that figure. **Not a question in this
