## CONSULT A -- REFUNDS AND THE STOREFRONT

*Priority: highest. A8 and A9 are the last things standing between us and
opening the store.*

### A7. Maine consumer protection *(carried forward, unanswered)*

Are there Maine statutes governing refunds on digital goods sold to consumers
that override anything in Section 9? And is the liability cap in **Section 11**
-- limited to the amount actually paid -- enforceable in Maine against a
consumer?

### A8. Section 10 is drafted from the seller's own description of itself. Is that safe? *(new)*

Section 10 is new and it exists because Gumroad told us, in writing, what
Gumroad's role is: merchant of record, the party the buyer pays, the party
that issues the receipt, collects and remits tax, and handles refunds and
chargebacks. We have written that into the agreement in plain words and
pointed the buyer at Gumroad's terms.

Two questions:

**(a)** Does naming Gumroad as merchant of record, and describing their role,
create any exposure for us -- for instance by appearing to make representations
on their behalf, or by binding us to a characterisation that Gumroad could
later change?

**(b)** Is it acceptable in a consumer agreement to incorporate a third
party's terms by link, when those terms can change without notice to either
the buyer or to us?

### A9. Selling the Guide with no refund at all. Is that defensible? *(new)*

Our reasoning is that every security setting the Guide covers is published in
full and free on our website before anyone buys, so the buyer can see exactly
what they are getting. On that basis the Guide is sold without a refund from
us, while Checkup and the bundle carry fourteen days.

**(a)** Is "you could read it all free first" an adequate basis for a
no-refund term on a digital good sold to a consumer?

**(b)** Section 9 also tells the buyer plainly that Gumroad may refund at its
own discretion regardless, and that nothing in our agreement removes rights
they hold under their local consumer law. **Does saying that weaken the
no-refund term to the point where we should not state it at all?**

**(c)** Does the EU/UK fourteen-day withdrawal right reach us through a
Gumroad sale, and if it does, is the waiver Gumroad presents at checkout
sufficient, or does something need to appear in our agreement?

## CONSULT B -- ENFORCEMENT AND PROTECTING THE WORK

*Priority: high. B1 has been outstanding since August 4.*

### B1. The cease-and-desist letter *(carried forward, unanswered)*

On August 4 you agreed we should have a template ready -- email first, U.S.
mail if there is no response -- and said you would supply the language. We
have not received it. **Can you provide it, or shall we draft it and send it
to you for review?**

### B2. We removed PC binding. Did we create a new problem? *(carried forward)*

The licence grants one PC, or three, five or ten for the multi-PC packs,
enforced by the terms and not by the software.

**(a)** Is a per-PC limit with no technical enforcement worth stating at all,
or does an unenforced restriction weaken the agreement generally?

**(b)** The multi-PC packs are priced tiers with nothing technical behind them.
Is there a defensible way to price them, or should they be dropped?

**(c)** You asked us to test the binding clause against Section 5 of the FTC
Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45. Removing binding appears to make that test moot. **Do you
agree, or does something survive the removal?**

### B3. Copying the Guide *(carried forward)*

Section 5 prohibits derivative works including rewritten, adapted, translated
and reformatted versions. Is there anything further worth adding, or is that
the practical ceiling?

### B4. Copyright registration *(carried forward, unanswered)*

For source-available software, what goes in the deposit -- full source, or the
redacted first-and-last-25-pages option? Register as published or unpublished,
given we have not launched? Checkup and the Guide as one work or two? And is
registration a prerequisite to suing someone who redistributes the code?

*This one does not need call time. A written answer is fine.*

### B5. Trademark *(carried forward, unanswered)*

"GatewayGuard" is the company and "GatewayGuard Checkup" is the product. Is a
federal filing worth the cost before there is revenue? Is there conflict risk
worth clearing first, particularly around the word **Checkup**, which is
descriptive and may be hard to protect?

*Written answer is fine.*

### B6. We tell the buyer the source code is readable, and it is. Does that cost us anything? *(new)*

Checkup ships as a PowerShell script. **The customer can open it in Notepad
and read every line**, and we say so in Sections 2, 3 and 8 because it is
central to why anyone should trust a security tool from a company they have
never heard of.

Sections 3 and 4 permit reading and quoting short portions with credit, and
prohibit posting it, modifying it, or putting our code into another product.

**Is "source-available but not open-source" a coherent position to hold in a
consumer licence, and are Sections 3 and 4 drafted tightly enough to keep it?**
We are specifically not offering an open-source licence and do not want to be
read as having done so by accident.

### C2. Should the programs review ship at launch? *(carried forward, unanswered)*

You approved the design on August 4 -- approval required on screen before any

### C3. Naming a real company's software as risky *(carried forward)*

Section 7 states that our recommendations are our opinion, based on our

opinion framing sufficient protection against a claim from a company whose
product we name? Is there a category of software we should never name?**

### C4. The annual Windows update *(carried forward)*

Section 8 tells the customer that Microsoft's yearly update may change or

**Does disclosing it create any obligation on us to actually provide an

*This matters more than it did in August: we now sell an annual update as a

### C5. Beta testers *(carried forward, unanswered, and now urgent)*

Testers receive a free licence in exchange for running Checkup on their own

## CONSULT D -- CONTRACT COMPLETENESS AND RUNNING THE BUSINESS

*Priority: medium. Most of these can be answered in writing.*

### D1. The five clauses we did not add *(carried forward)*

Of the six clauses discussed on August 4, our notes record only severability,
now **Section 14**. **One more has since been drafted on our own initiative:
Section 13, a mechanism for changing terms in future versions** -- see D2.

Still absent: **arbitration, entire agreement, assignment, and age and export
restrictions.** For a $19.99 consumer download, which are worth having?

### D2. Section 13 is ours, drafted from scratch. Confirm or replace it. *(revised)*

It reads that we may change the terms; that the current version is always
posted at gatewayguard.co/license; and that **a change never applies backwards
-- the terms in force when you bought stay in force for that purchase, and a
later purchase takes the terms posted at that time.**

It is deliberately narrow. **Is it drafted correctly for that intent, and is
"posted at a URL" adequate notice for a consumer?**

### D3. Is the severability clause adequate? *(carried forward, renumbered)*

**Section 14** reads: "If a court decides that any part of this agreement is
invalid, illegal, or cannot be enforced, that part is removed and the rest of
the agreement stays in full effect."

Some severability clauses reach only provisions that are outright illegal,
others reach anything unenforceable. **Is ours at the right scope?**

### D4. Section 15, and the carve-out we removed *(new -- was a v3.1 marker)*

v3.0 read: *"Ending the licence does not entitle you to a refund except as
described in Section 9."* That pointed a customer terminated for breach at an
unconditional fourteen-day right.

**We removed the carve-out**, on the reasoning that a breach will not come to
light inside fourteen days and that refunds are Gumroad's to decide anyway.
Section 15 now simply points to Section 9.

**Confirm that dropping it is acceptable, or supply wording.**

### D5. Two answers from August 4 we could not act on *(carried forward)*

**(a)** On the question of a minimum, the answer recorded was that there is no
minimum and we can set our own. **A minimum of what** -- purchase quantity,
licence term, or price?

**(b)** One answer was referred to LegalZoom with the note "qualify in terms
of," and the sentence was not finished. **What was the qualification?**

### D6. Emailing every past customer when an annual update ships *(carried forward)*

Our reading of the CAN-SPAM Rule, 16 CFR Part 316, is that an email containing
only warranty, safety or security information about a product the recipient
bought, or notice of a change in terms, is transactional or relationship
content and largely exempt -- but that the FTC reads those categories narrowly,
and that including a sales pitch for the paid update makes the whole message
commercial.

**Our notice will promote the paid update.** Should we simply build to the
full commercial standard from the start -- opt-out link, advertisement
identification, physical postal address? **And does holding a customer email
list for this purpose require a published privacy policy?**

### D7. Privacy policy *(carried forward)*

Hardware fingerprinting is gone, but we will hold customer email addresses.
**Do we need a published privacy policy for a product that collects nothing
from the user's PC but keeps a purchase and contact record?**

### D8. Maine sales tax *(carried forward)*

We are asking Maine Revenue Services directly about registering to collect and
are not asking you to determine taxability. **We want to understand our
exposure if we get it wrong for the first several months, and whether that is
a question for you or for a CPA.** Note that Gumroad states it acts as merchant
of record and handles sales tax collection and remittance.

### D9. The question we still have not asked *(carried forward)*

We put this to you on August 4 and did not reach it. **You have now read the
agreement twice and you know the business. What is the largest legal risk in
this launch that we have not raised in any of these questions?**
