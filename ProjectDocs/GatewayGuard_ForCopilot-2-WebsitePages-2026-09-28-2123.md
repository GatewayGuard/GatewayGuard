Edge Password Saving
Edge offers to save your passwords so it can fill them in automatically. It is convenient, but it stores all your passwords in one place tied to your browser — if your PC is compromised, every saved password goes with it.
▶ Your decision
What Checkup found and we recommend you do
Found: Checkup reviews Edge’s password saving and autofill. It can turn that off with your permission — and only if you have told Checkup you already use a separate password manager.
Action taken: With your approval, Checkup will turn off Edge’s offer to save passwords. This does not delete any passwords already saved — it only stops Edge from offering to save new ones going forward.
Why you might want this off
A browser password manager is a convenient single point of failure. If someone gets into your Windows account — either by sitting down at your PC or by malware running on it — they can export every password Edge has saved in a few clicks. All your banking, email, and shopping accounts go at once.
A dedicated password manager like Bitwarden (free) or 1Password keeps your passwords in an encrypted vault that requires its own separate master password to open. Even if someone gets into your Windows account, they still cannot get into your password vault without that second password.
How to check it yourself
Open Microsoft Edge .
Click the three dots ( ⋯ ) in the top right, then click Settings .
Click Passwords in the left sidebar.
Confirm Offer to save passwords is set to Off .
How to turn it back on if you want it
Open Edge › Settings › Passwords.
Toggle Offer to save passwords to On .
Already have passwords saved in Edge? Turning off the offer to save does not delete them. To see or delete saved passwords, go to Edge › Settings › Passwords and scroll down to the list. You can export them from there before deleting if you want to move them to a dedicated password manager.
What to expect
Edge will no longer pop up asking if you want to save a password when you log into a website. Any passwords already saved in Edge continue to autofill as before until you delete them. No other feature of Edge is affected.
Recommended free alternative: Bitwarden. Bitwarden is a free, open-source password manager that works in Edge, Chrome, Firefox, and on your phone. It stores your passwords in an encrypted vault and fills them in automatically — with the added protection of a separate master password. Visit bitwarden.com to get started.

Found: Checkup reviews all three Enhanced Phishing Protection settings. It can turn them on with your permission, or show you the exact steps if Windows blocks the change.

Why this matters
Phishing is one of the most reported online crimes against older adults. In 2025, people aged 60 and over filed more than 200,000 complaints with the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center, and phishing and spoofing were among the most reported. A fake website that looks like your bank, a pop-up asking you to sign in, an email link that takes you to a convincing copy of a real site — the goal is the same every time: get your password.
Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, 2025 Internet Crime Report .
These three settings give Windows awareness of when your Windows password is being typed somewhere risky, and alert you before you accidentally hand it over.
How to check it yourself
Open Windows Security and click App & browser control .
Click Reputation-based protection settings .
Scroll down to the Phishing protection section.
Confirm all three are On : Warn me about malicious apps and sites , Warn me about password reuse , and Warn me about unsafe password storage .
How to change it yourself
Open Windows Security > App & browser control > Reputation-based protection settings .
Under Phishing protection , turn on Warn me about malicious apps and sites , Warn me about password reuse , and Warn me about unsafe password storage .
Leave Automatically collect website or app content when additional analysis is needed to help identify security threats unchecked. It is a separate checkbox in the same section that sends more of your screen to Microsoft than these three warnings need — Checkup does not set it either way.
What to expect
No restart required. The protection is active immediately. You’ll only notice it if you type your Windows password somewhere it flags as risky — you’ll see a pop-up warning.
Note: These settings protect your Windows sign-in password . For protecting passwords to websites and online accounts, a dedicated password manager (such as Bitwarden, which is free) is the right tool for that job.

Windows Widgets
Windows Widgets is the news, weather, and sports panel that appears on your taskbar. It sends information about what you read and click back to Microsoft. Turning it off removes the weather button from the left of your taskbar, so the headlines and alerts it carries stop appearing there. Found: Checkup reviews Windows Widgets on your taskbar. It can turn it off with your permission.
Action taken: With your approval, Checkup will turn off Windows Widgets. If you use and enjoy the Widgets panel, you can turn it back on at any time — the steps are below.
Why you might want this off
Widgets sends your reading and browsing habits back to Microsoft. The panel is powered by Microsoft Start, and it tracks which stories you read, how long you spend on them, and what you click on — to serve you more personalized content. If you do not want Microsoft collecting that information, turning Widgets off is the clean solution.
There is also a background process involved. Widgets uses the Edge browser engine (WebView2) to run even when you are not looking at it. Turning it off frees up that memory and removes a process that was running without you asking for it.
How to check it yourself
Press the Windows key , type Taskbar settings , and press Enter .
Look for Widgets in the list. If the toggle is Off , Widgets is disabled.
How to turn it back on if you want it
Press the Windows key , type Taskbar settings , and press Enter .
Toggle Widgets to On .
The Widgets button reappears on your taskbar immediately. No restart needed.
If the Widgets switch is greyed out and you cannot click it, Checkup turned Widgets off for the whole PC rather than just for you, and Windows will not let the ordinary setting override that. Tell us and we will give you the exact steps to undo it. Nothing is lost — it is a single value that has to be removed, and your PC restarted once.
What to expect
With Widgets off, the weather and news icon disappears from your taskbar. Your PC will use slightly less memory in the background. Everything else on your PC works exactly the same. If you want news or weather, your browser works just as well for that.
This is more than a taste. The panel builds a record of what you read and click, and Microsoft says it becomes more personalised the longer you use it. Checkup turns it off with your permission, and you can turn it back on whenever you like.

Your PC is protected from the moment you turn it on — but only if Defender is running. Real-time protection scans every file you open, every download, and every program you run. Without it, malware can install itself before anything notices.

Diagnostic Data
Windows sends information about how your PC is running back to Microsoft. There are two levels: the minimum needed to keep Windows working, and a broader set that includes information about what you do on your PC. Checkup recommends the minimum.
▶ Your decision
What Checkup found and we recommend you do
Found: Checkup reviews how much diagnostic data your PC sends to Microsoft. It can limit this to the required minimum with your permission.
Action taken: With your approval, Checkup will set Diagnostic data to Required diagnostic data . This is the minimum level Windows needs to function and receive updates. It does not affect performance or updates in any way.
Why you might want the minimum
The optional level sends information about what you do on your PC. This includes which apps you use, how long you use them, which features you click on, and details about your browsing. Microsoft uses this to improve Windows — but it is your PC and your data, and you may not want to share it.
The required level sends only what Windows needs to work: basic device information, error reports, and update-related data. Your PC gets the same updates either way. The difference is how much information Microsoft receives about your daily use.
How to set it to the minimum
Press the Windows key , type Diagnostics & feedback , and press Enter .
Under Diagnostic data , you will see two options: Required diagnostic data and Optional diagnostic data .
Select Required diagnostic data . It should already be selected if Checkup ran successfully. If it shows Optional, click Required to change it.
What to expect
No restart required. Windows continues to receive all updates and works exactly the same. The change is that Microsoft receives less information about how you use your PC.
This is more than a taste. Nobody is reading your day — but a record of what you did on your own computer is kept where you cannot see it, for as long as someone decides to keep it, and you were never asked. Turning it down does not erase what is already there; it stops the list getting longer. Checkup makes this change with your permission.

Advertising ID
Windows assigns your PC a unique tracking number that apps use to follow your activity and show you targeted ads. Turning it off removes that number and stops apps from tracking you across different programs.
▶ Your decision
What Checkup found and we recommend you do
Found: Checkup reviews your Advertising ID setting, which lets apps show you personalized ads. It can turn that off with your permission.
Action taken: With your approval, Checkup will turn off the Advertising ID. You can turn it back on at any time — the steps are below.
Why you might want this off
The Advertising ID is a tracking number Microsoft assigns to your PC. Apps that use it can see what you do across different programs and use that information to target you with ads. Think of it like a loyalty card number — except you did not sign up for it, and the data goes to advertisers you have never heard of.
Turning it off does not stop you from seeing ads. It just means the ads you see are not based on tracking what you do on your own PC. This is more than a taste: a record of what you do, built on your own machine and handed to advertisers you have never heard of, stops getting longer the moment you turn it off.
How to turn it off
Press the Windows key , type Settings , and press Enter .
Select Privacy & security .
Scroll down — or use the down arrow key or the Page Down key — and select Recommendations and offers .
Find Advertising ID — Let apps show me personalized ads by using my advertising ID . The toggle should be Off . If it is On, click it to turn it Off.
How to turn it back on if you want it
Press the Windows key , type Settings , and press Enter .
Select Privacy & security .
Scroll down — or use the down arrow key or the Page Down key — and select Recommendations and offers .
Find Advertising ID — Let apps show me personalized ads by using my advertising ID and click the toggle to turn it On .
What to expect
With the Advertising ID off, apps can no longer track your activity across programs to serve targeted ads. Your PC works exactly the same in every other way. No restart is needed. If you use apps from the Microsoft Store, you may notice ads become less targeted over time — that is the expected result.
This is more than a taste. The tracking number lets apps build a record of what you do across programs and hand it to advertisers. Checkup turns it off with your permission, and you can turn it back on whenever you like.
