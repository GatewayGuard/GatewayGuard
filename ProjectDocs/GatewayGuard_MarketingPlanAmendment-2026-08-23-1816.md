contradiction rather than a fix.

### 4c. Section 2 — add the UNIT RULE beneath the BANNED table

```
### THE UNIT RULE -- every price carries its unit, every time

**$12.99 names two different products**: the Security Guide, bought once, and
the Checkup annual update, per PC, per year. Write **"$12.99 once"** or
**"$12.99 a year"**. **A bare "$12.99" with no unit is a defect wherever it

The same applies to every figure: the four one-time pack prices take *once*,

**This is not the banned "buy it once".** That phrase is a promise about how
often a buyer will be asked for money. *Once* attached to a price is a unit,
and removing it would leave bare figures that collide.
```

## 5. THE ANNUAL UPDATE — WHAT IT IS, AND ONE CLAIM THAT CANNOT BE MADE

### 5a. Section 4 or section 3 — add the strategy

**Bill, 2026-08-23:** each year there will be an updated Checkup that (1)
checks the settings against Microsoft's latest yearly Windows update and
corrects them where needed, with the user's approval, and (2) **rescans the
PC's hard drives.**

```
**What the annual update is.** Once a year Microsoft makes a major update to
Windows 11 -- settings move, new ones appear, old ones are retired. After that
lands, we publish a refreshed Checkup that knows where everything is in the new
version. It walks the settings again, offers to correct what the update changed
with the user's approval, and scans the drives again while it is there.

**The second half is what makes the first half sellable.** A yearly charge for
a settings refresh is thin. A yearly charge for a settings refresh AND a fresh
scan of the machine is a yearly checkup, which is what the product is named
after.
```

### 5b. **A CONSTRAINT, NOT COPY — Claude Code, read this before any of it ships**

**The drive scan is not built.** F4, the second drive, route 3, is in
ascii43's *not built* list, and **its screen text is gate-24-blocked until a
full scan is measured actually covering `D:` on SANDY.** The strategy above
depends on a capability the current build cannot demonstrate.

**And its wording is already constrained by a measurement.** Measured
2026-08-21: `Start-MpWDOScan` has no scope parameter at all, so drive coverage
comes from `Start-MpScan -ScanType FullScan`, a full **online** scan. **The
copy must say "scans your drives" or "a full scan of all your drives", and
must never say "offline scan".**

**"Corrects" has two exceptions.** Tamper Protection and Windows Hello carry
`CanAuto=false` — Checkup cannot change them. The claim is true for 17 of 19.

### 5c. **A CLAIM THAT CANNOT BE MADE — measured against ascii43, 2026-08-23**

**Checkup does not remember what you approved last time, and does not report
what changed since.** Measured in
`Tool\W11-SecurityHardening-v3-ascii43-2026-08-21-1752.ps1`:

| What exists | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Test-FirstRun` / `$FirstRunFlag` | Knows *that* you have run before. Nothing about what happened |
| `Save-Checkpoint`, `$CheckpointOrder`, `Show-ResumePrompt` | Nine ordered checkpoints, to survive the offline-scan reboot. **A resume mechanism for one interrupted session** |
| State file line 2 | **One** saved answer — the password-manager response, so a resume does not ask a third time. FT-127 |
| The log | What was found and changed, `Was: ... -> Now:` per item, with a double-clickable opener written beside it every launch. FT-189 |

**So this may be written:** *every run writes a plain-English record of what it
found and what you approved, and you can open it at any time.*

**This may NOT be written until it is built:** *it remembers the choices you
approved last time and tells you if anything has changed since.*

**That second version is worth building and is the strongest argument for the
yearly update.** It needs a saved record of approved settings and a comparison
pass. **It belongs in FutureProjects with an FP number** — Claude Code's to
file. *(Open, and one look settles it: is the checkpoint cleared at the end of
a normal completed run?)*

## 7. COMMENTARY — THREE THINGS TO SETTLE, NONE OF THEM MINE

*Not blocks.*

**1. Editions.** *Buy it once* is gone, but the incongruity it named has been
managed rather than solved. **The clean solution is year-named editions** —
*Checkup 2026* at $19.99 once, *Checkup 2027* at $12.99 for owners. Nobody
expects a product with a year in its name to also be next year's product.
**Two knock-ons:** `CLAUDE.md` says the version is *always v3.1 in all
user-facing text* (Bill, 2026-08-15), which a year-named edition contradicts;
and the edition year would need to track the Windows release rather than the
calendar, or a September launch is behind Windows by October.

**2. The skipped-year question.** Nothing anywhere says what happens if a buyer
skips two years and then wants to be current — newest update at $12.99 a year,
or the years they missed. **A store listing will be asked this.** It belongs
with decision 3.

**3. The bundle's terms.** *One Checkup licence for one PC plus one Guide
licence* is the obvious reading and **nobody has written it down.** There is
also no bundle for the 3, 5 and 10-PC packs. The licence draft has promised a
bundle since 2026-08-04 and only got a price today.
