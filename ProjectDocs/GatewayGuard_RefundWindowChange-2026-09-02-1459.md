## THE ONE THING THAT HAS TO BE SETTLED FIRST

**The licence does not have a Checkup refund clause. It has ONE clause covering
both products.** ***sourced, `License-2026-08-25-1400-TEXT.md`, Section 9:***

> *"**Checkup and the Guide** are downloadable files. If **either one** is not
> what you expected, write to us within **30 days** of buying and we will refund
> you in full. You do not have to give a reason."*

**So "Checkup is 14 days" cannot be applied as a single edit.** One of two
things has to happen, and only Bill can choose:

| | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Both products go to 14 days** | One number changes in one sentence. The clause stays as one clause | **Smallest.** One edit, one attorney note |
| **B. Checkup 14, Guide 30** | Section 9 splits into two paragraphs. Two windows to state on the website, in the two Gumroad settings, and in the receipt | Larger, but defensible -- see below |

**Asked of Bill 2026-09-02. Not assumed here.**

**There is a real argument for B.** They are different purchases. The Guide is
read; Checkup is run. A buyer knows within an evening whether a PDF is what they
wanted, but may not get round to running a security tool on their PC for two
weeks. **If anything, that argues the longer window belongs to Checkup**, which
is the opposite of what a shorter Checkup window does.

## ONE FACT FOR THE ATTORNEY, NOT AN OBJECTION

***sourced, EU Consumer Rights Directive:*** consumers buying at a distance have
a statutory **14-day right of withdrawal**. For digital content delivered
immediately that right can be waived, but only with the buyer's express prior
consent and acknowledgement.

**So a 14-day window sits exactly on the statutory floor rather than above it.**
At 30 days there was margin; at 14 there is none, and the licence already
promises *"Nothing here takes away any right you have under the consumer law
where you live."*

***Not measured: how many buyers will be outside the US.*** GatewayGuard is
written for American seniors and the answer is probably "almost none" -- which
is why this is **a line for the consult question list, not a reason to
reconsider.** It costs nothing to have the attorney confirm it.

**Also worth knowing:** Section 9 says Gumroad *"may also issue a refund under
its own policy, which can run longer than our 30 days."* **That sentence still
works at 14 days** -- the gap simply widens. ***Not measured: what Gumroad's own
refund window actually is.*** Worth settling, because a buyer refused at day 20
by us and granted at day 20 by Gumroad is a support problem we would rather see
coming.
